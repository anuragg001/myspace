###

I didn't expect to be nervous about a hackathon.

But there I was at Parul University, walking in with no clue what the problem statement would be.

Just another Saturday that turned into something I won't forget.

####

### The Beginning (Also Known As Free Stuff)

They handed us goodies when we walked in.

Stickers.  
A notebook.  
A pencil.

Honestly? I was more excited about the breakfast.

Real coffee.  
Real food.  
The kind that makes you feel like you're actually supposed to be somewhere important.

That breakfast hit different when you're about to spend the next 24 hours sitting in front of a screen.

####

### The Problem Statement Was... A Choice

Two options.

**Option 1:** A POS system for a coffee shop.  
Straightforward. Familiar. Probably the "smart" choice.

**Option 2:** An ERP system for a furniture shop.  
Complex. Messy. Overwhelming.

We looked at each other.

And we chose the complicated one.

I'm still not sure if that was brave or stupid. Probably both.

An ERP system meant inventory management, order handling, supplier tracking, customer data. The whole thing. Not just "take an order, make coffee." 

But that's what made it interesting.

####

### Hour 1-6: The Confusion Phase

The first few hours were just... reading the requirements.

Reading them again.  
Understanding what we actually needed to build.  
Arguing about the approach.

No code yet.  
Just planning.  
And coffee.

Lots of coffee.

We had to think about:
- How furniture orders flow through the system
- Inventory tracking for different materials
- Customer management
- Supplier coordination

On paper, it looked impossible.

In reality? Still impossible, but at least we had a plan now.

####

### The Mentor Sessions Were Actually Helpful

Here's the thing about hackathons I didn't expect:

The mentors don't give you the answer.

They just ask the right questions.

"Have you thought about how you'll handle concurrent orders?"

"What happens when inventory runs out?"

"Are you overcomplicating the database structure?"

And suddenly you're thinking differently.

Less about showing off.  
More about actually solving the problem.

####

### Lunch Was Better Than Expected

By hour 12, we were tired.

Hungry.  
Delirious.  
Making decisions that seemed brilliant at the time and questionable now.

Then lunch arrived.

Not pizza.  
Not sandwiches.  

Actual, proper food.

It was one of those moments where food doesn't taste like fuel anymore. It tastes like hope. Like maybe we could actually finish this.

We ate.  
We coded.  
We complained about database design.  
Repeat.

####

### Hour 12-20: The Building Madness

This is where the real work happened.

APIs.  
Database schemas.  
Frontend components.  
Integration with Odoo.

The hours blurred together.

Someone made a terrible joke about database migrations.  
Someone else almost fell asleep at the keyboard.  
I refactored the same component four times because it just didn't feel right.

But we were moving.

Slow.  
Chaotic.  
But definitely moving.

The code wasn't perfect.  
It wasn't even close to perfect.

But it worked.

####

### The Last Hours (AKA Panic Mode)

With 4 hours left, we realized we had more features planned than time to build them.

So we cut things.  
Aggressively.

The beautiful dashboard we dreamed about?  
Cut.

The advanced analytics?  
Cut.

The extra validations?  
Also cut.

What we kept was the core:  
A system that actually manages a furniture shop's ERP needs.

Inventory works.  
Orders flow through.  
Customers can be tracked.  
Suppliers can be managed.

Everything else could wait.

####

### The Moment We Finished

There's something weird about finishing a hackathon.

You spent 24 hours pushing.  
Stressing.  
Building.

And then suddenly... it's done.

The demo was 5 minutes.  
We showed what we built.  
Explained the choices we made.  
And sat back down.

We didn't win.

But we didn't expect to either.

What we got was different.

A working ERP system.  
A team that figured out how to work together under pressure.  
Proof that we could handle something complex and actually ship it.

####

### What I Took Away

It wasn't about winning.

It was about:

Realizing that planning takes time.  
That mentorship actually works.  
That sometimes you have to cut features to ship something real.  
That your team matters more than your code.

And that 24 hours is both impossibly short and somehow enough.

If you try hard enough.  
And make better choices than bad ones.  

Odoo hackathon 2026. Parul University.  
Not bad for a Saturday.
